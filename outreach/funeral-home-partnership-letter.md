# Infinity Map — Funeral Home Partnership Letter

Email-ready outreach letter for greater Cincinnati funeral homes. Personalize the
bracketed fields before sending. Pairs with the contact list in
`cincinnati-funeral-home-contacts.md`.

**Deliberately no link and no attachment.** The letter highlights what the
Infinity Map covers but does not hand over the workbook itself — funeral homes
must reply by email or call/text Michael at (513) 850-3570 to see it. This
protects the material from being copied and turns every response into a
conversation.

**Subject:** A free tool that makes arrangement conferences easier for the families you serve

Dear [Funeral Director's Name / the team at Funeral Home Name],

My name is Michael Myers. I'm a pastor here in the greater Cincinnati area and
lead HomeTown Ministries International. Over the years I've been asked to
officiate a lot of funerals — which means I've sat with a lot of families in
those first hours, helping them organize their thoughts about a whole life in a
very short amount of time. I'm writing because that experience led me to build
something I believe could genuinely help the families who walk through your
doors — and I'd love to show it to you.

You know better than anyone what it looks like when a family arrives to make
arrangements completely unprepared. No one wrote down Mom's Social Security
number, her mother's maiden name, or where the insurance policies are. No one
knows whether Dad wanted burial or cremation, which hymn mattered to him, or who
should be called first. Grieving families end up making dozens of decisions in
their hardest hours with none of the information in front of them — and your
staff carries the weight of walking them through it.

That's the gap we built **The Infinity Map** to close — and it was written from
the pastor's chair. Sitting at kitchen tables with grieving families has taught
me the questions that surface there but often never make it into the paperwork:
the story behind how they met, what he'd want said in the eulogy — and what he
wouldn't — who should read the scripture, which songs must not be played. The
Infinity Map asks those questions right alongside the practical ones, so it
complements the details your team gathers with the side of the conversation
families usually save for their pastor.

The Infinity Map is a comprehensive life-and-legacy planning workbook — our
tagline is *"Because love plans ahead."* Across 22 guided sections, a person
records everything their family will need before the hardest day comes.
Highlights of what a completed Infinity Map puts in your arrangement conference:

- **All 33 fields of the U.S. Standard Certificate of Death, pre-filled** — the
  information you're required to file within 72 hours, ready on day one
- **Documented funeral and burial wishes** — disposition, casket or urn
  preferences, viewing, clothing, flowers, even the reception
- **Complete service planning** — officiant, music, scripture, eulogy,
  pallbearers, and obituary notes already decided by the person themselves
- **A notification list and key contacts** — so the family knows exactly who to
  call, in what order
- Plus medical directives, financial and insurance overviews, asset
  distribution, and an entire legacy half — life story, letters to loved ones,
  faith statement, and recorded life wisdom

When a family completes an Infinity Map ahead of time, they arrive at your door
with the paperwork answered and far fewer impossible decisions to make on the
spot. It makes your team's work smoother, shortens difficult conferences, and
naturally opens pre-need conversations — because a family that has filled out an
Infinity Map has already started planning.

## Sending notes

- **No link, no attachment — on purpose.** The workbook goes out only after they
  respond, so it can't be quietly copied and reproduced.
- **Personalize the first line** with the director's name and one specific detail
  (e.g., "your family has served Price Hill for four generations") — funeral
  homes are relationship businesses and generic blasts get deleted.
- **Send individually**, not as a mass BCC. A handful a day from your normal
  Gmail account reads as genuine and avoids spam filtering.
- **Follow up once** after about a week, ideally by phone — funeral directors
  answer their phones.
- Each message includes your real name, organization, and contact info, and is a
  one-time business inquiry to a publicly advertised business address — but if
  any home asks not to be contacted again, honor it immediately.
